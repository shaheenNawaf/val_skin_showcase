P0 · SHIPPED AND UNREACHABLE

The tier filter toolbar is behind the card.
All 14 of its controls. Completely.

The "one-click tier presets" feature from commits 389432f and 78ed6bf — presets, six tier chips, the per-category cap, Apply, Reset, the count — is not visible and not clickable anywhere in the editor. I proved it three ways: elementFromPoint() hit-testing at each control's centre returns #stage, #card and a panel <h3>; a screenshot with a bright green outline on the toolbar contains no green anywhere; and the card's own skin-remove × buttons are at a lower z-index than the stage, so they work only by accident of stacking.

The cause is one line of CSS: .tierbar is a static in-flow block, #stage is position:fixed with an opaque background, and --tbh is measured from the topbar alone. Positioned elements paint after in-flow blocks, so the stage covers the toolbar's band. Its hard-coded margin:64px cannot escape a fixed sibling.

This has been live the whole time the feature has existed. It is also why "the tier presets do not seem to work" would be the natural thing for a seller to say and impossible for them to justify — there is nothing to click.

The one open decision: what to do about a 1920px card on a small screen

The card is a fixed 1920×1080 canvas that gets scaled to fit. The code floors the scale at 0.35, so on a 390 px phone the card renders at 35% and its 9 px text becomes 3.1 px. There is no zoom control anywhere, so a phone buyer simply cannot read the card. On a 1366×768 laptop it lands at 60%, which turns 9 px into 5.4 px. These are the four ways out. Prototype 5 lets you try each one at six viewports.

A

ADD A ZOOM CONTROL

Cheapest. ~15 lines of code.

Leave the card exactly as it is, and add Fit / 100% / 200% / open full size buttons so the buyer can magnify and pan. The card stays a faithful thumbnail by default.

  • Costs: nothing in the design
  • Gets you: everything, if the buyer taps zoom
  • Leaves: the default view still unreadable
  • Effort: hours
B

RE-DRAW THE KEY TEXT ON TOP

Best result. Most work.

The canvas still scales, but the six fields a buyer actually reads — price, rank, seller, status, skin count, title — are re-rendered as crisp HTML pinned over their canvas coordinates, never smaller than 16 px. Everything else stays an image.

  • Costs: a coordinate map per theme; hide the overlay on export
  • Gets you: every field readable at every viewport
  • Leaves: the skin art still small, which is fine
  • Effort: days
C

DESIGN BIGGER TEXT INTO THE CARD

Improves the exported PNG too.

Change nothing about scaling. Instead, author nothing below 13 px in the 1920 px design, so the smallest text is 13 px before scaling rather than 9 px. On a laptop 5.4 px becomes 7.8 px.

  • Costs: the exported PNG changes, so old listings look different from their images
  • Gets you: better everywhere, and a better artefact to post
  • Leaves: phones still at 4.5 px — still unreadable
  • Effort: a day, mostly re-checking panel fits
D

GO NATIVE ON PHONES

Stop drawing a canvas at all.

Below 900 px, throw the canvas away and render the same data as an ordinary mobile page: title and price at real size, then panels as stacked rows with 16 px skin names. The 1920 canvas is only used where there is room for it.

  • Costs: two rendering paths to keep in sync
  • Gets you: a genuinely good phone experience
  • Leaves: the laptop and tablet problem untouched
  • Effort: days — the viewer half-exists already below 700 px
Why I am not just picking one. Each fixes a different thing: A is free, C improves the PNG people actually post, B is the only one that fully solves it, and D is the only one that makes phones first-class. They also stack — A and C together is most of the benefit for about a day's work.
What I would do. A + C now, because they are cheap, independent, and cover the laptop case properly. Then B if the buyer path is where you want to spend effort, since it is the only option that leaves nothing unreadable. D only if phones are a priority you have decided to fund.

Method, so you can check our work

  • • Two independent passes. The first audited the editor, viewer, marketplace and layout engine. The second re-verified the first's headline claims from scratch and audited the surfaces the first did not open: the share/SEO head, the publish and import funnels, the schema's integrity guarantees, and the bytes actually on the wire.
